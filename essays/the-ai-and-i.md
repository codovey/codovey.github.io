# The AI that I Did (or Didn't) Use
As per the assignment guidelines, I should explain how I used AI for each topic provided. I may be in software engineering, but I am an artist and environmental advocate, two things that don't mix well with AI. Because of that, I avoided using AI like the plague unless I felt I really truly needed it. Have a look into my failed attempts at making Microsoft Copilot my copilot.

### Experience WODs e.g. E18
For the experiences I did complete, I looked at the tutorial solution videos first so I wouldn't have to consult the AI chatbots. It isn't recommended to watch the videos first, but taking a quick peek when I got stuck helped a lot. AI probably could have worked, but I didn't feel the need to use it most times.

### In-class Practice WODs + In-class (Not Practice!) WODs
In these cases, AI gave me the most grief. My two highlights are from the In-class Wet Bulb Global Temperature WOD and the In-class Cornhole WOD. I tried to ask AI what was wrong with my code and told it what I wanted instead. That didn't work. My code stayed broken and I lost many WOD points. I'll elaborate more in future specific sections.

### Essays
Personally, I could never use AI for writing. I may be a terrible writer at times, but I wouldn't ask AI to do the task without improving the quality much. AI writing uses em-dashes and comparisons too often. I can usually come up with my own ideas, so I wouldn't need AI for that either. I prefer to have each piece I write entirely my own work.

### Final project
I probably could have asked AI to help me with my specific final project issues, but it has disappointed me too many times over the course of the semester for me to even try. My teammates may have used AI, and if it helped them, that's great! I didn't want to waste resources on something AI wouldn't benefit me with or something I could probably do myself.

### Learning a concept / tutorial
I didn't consider using AI for this, but it could have helped if I asked.

### Answering a question in class or in Discord
I'm going to be honest here. I was never the one answering questions. I was always the one asking. I'm often clueless when things start breaking (**POSTGRES. IT WAS ALMOST ALWAYS POSTGRES GIVING ME ISSUES.**), so I tried to ask others for help whenever possible. I probably should have done that more instead of missing assignments I thought were difficult...

### Asking or answering a smart-question
I saved asking smart-questions for Stack Overflow. 

### Coding example e.g. “give an example of using Underscore .pluck”
I think I searched on Microsoft Edge specific HTML formatting, like how to fit text on top of images, and the AI pointed me in a slightly helpful direction. I completed both the In-Class and Practice WODs with flying colors, so I'm not disappointed in this specific scenario.

### Explaining code
I never asked AI to explain any code, but I'm sure it's capable of that. AI runs on code, so it should be able to understand it.

### Writing code
I tried this once during the Cornhole WOD and regretted it terribly. I wasn't so great at making object declarations, so I took a couple of the little tasks from the WOD and asked Copilot to help me write a bit of code. This led to code that didn't calculate the final score properly. Surely I could have asked it to fix some of my bugged code, right? Wrong! I'll elaborate more in the Quality assurance section.

### Documenting code
When I document code, I mostly leave short little notes. I don't use AI for that specific writing purpose anyway.

### Quality assurance e.g. “What’s wrong with this code <code here>” or “Fix the ESLint errors in <code here>”
The beef I had with AI increased tenfold after the previously mentioned Wet Bulb and Cornhole WODS. My dearest Copilot, please tell me what's wrong with my code! She lies to me with every virtual breath she takes. 

For the Wet Bulb in-class WOD, I asked why my formula for calculating the wet bulb global temperature wasn't working. It handed me new equations, but they didn't work. Surprise. Copilot didn't tell me the issue was my units were in Fahrenheit. I only figured that out once the timer ended. It was a terrible day after that. I missed out on so many points!

As for the Cornhole WOD, Copilot killed all my faith in using AI for software engineering. Everyone had their code working except me. Why? I couldn't get my code to calculate points the proper cornhole way. In cornhole, you're supposed to use the difference in points as the final score for the team in the lead and give the other team 0. I asked AI for help and it tried to gaslight me into thinking giving each team their total points was the correct option. I tried prompting multiple times to explain how cornhole points worked, but it refused to fix my code properly. Never have I ever been more disappointed.

### Other uses in ICS 314 not listed
The only "AI" that I think I utilized was VScode's automatic suggestions. Sometimes, it gave me good tips to plop into my already existing code. Other times, it gave silly suggestions. **It's always important to know whether the tips AI gives you are useful or trash.**

# What do I think of AI over all?
It sucks. Usually. If someone can get their favorite chatbot to complete a task exactly the way they want it, good for them! I'm glad it benefitted someone. Personally, I don't care much for AI since I don't go out of my way to use it that often. Especially after the disappointment and loss of points it has caused me. Pro tip: Use it only when you really need it, make sure it's correct, and do your thing. If I ever have to retake this class, I'll at least know which chatbot not to waste my time with. Microsoft Copilot.
